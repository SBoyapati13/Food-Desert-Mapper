How this is measured

  • Stores. USDA's Historical SNAP Retailer Locator data. A full-service store is one USDA classes as a supermarket, super store or large grocery store; it counts as open between its SNAP authorization date and end date. Records within 50 m of each other are one site. Stores in counties within 10 km of the state line count too.
  • Walking. Census TIGER/Line 2024 streets, service drives, walkways, stairs, alleys and paths, excluding limited-access highways and ramps; the same network every year. TIGER does not include some river-bridge walkways (for example the Ben Franklin and George Washington bridges), so those crossings are not counted.
  • People. Each block group is represented by its 2020 population-weighted centre. Households without a vehicle, population, income and poverty come from the ACS 2020–2024 5-year estimates; block-group estimates have wide margins of error, shown in the detail panel.
  • One-store areas. Block groups within a 1-mile walk of a full-service store but more than a mile from the next-nearest one, so a single closure would put them beyond a mile.
  • Lower-income block groups have a median household income at or below 80% of the state median, or 20% or more of people below the poverty line.
  • Limits. Stores that never took SNAP are missing. TIGER does not map sidewalks, so a street may count as walkable when it isn't pleasant or safe to walk. The measure is "low walking access" by one definition, not a judgement that an area is a food desert.
